The Curiosity Club. Episode 9. Eight Arms and Three Hearts.

Here is something to listen for. Why was the octopus difficult to see at first?

Ready? Let us find out.

Maya and Leo visit an aquarium. A sign says that an octopus lives in the tank in front of them. They can see rocks, water, and a small crab. They cannot see an octopus.

Perhaps it went shopping, Leo says. With eight arms, it could carry all the bags.

The keeper points to a shape beside a rock. Watch closely.

The shape slowly moves. A brown arm uncurls, and two eyes appear. The octopus has been there all along.

Its color looked like the rocks, says Maya.

The keeper explains that many octopuses can change their color and the appearance of their skin. That helps them blend into their surroundings.

Pip tries to hide by standing beside a gray trash can.

We can still see your flashing lights, Leo tells him.

The octopus crawls into a sheltered space. The crab moves away in another direction.

Does it have eight hearts for its eight arms? Maya asks.

Three hearts, says the keeper. Two send blood through the gills, and one sends it around the rest of the body.

Pip counts on his metal fingers. Eight arms, three hearts, and excellent hiding skills.

Would you like eight arms? Leo asks Maya.

Only until it is time to put on my jacket, she replies. Finding eight sleeves every morning would take forever.

Now, a little more to discover.

An octopus has eight arms and three hearts. Its gills take oxygen from water. Many octopuses use changes in color and skin texture as camouflage. These features belong to the animal's biology; the shopping trip and eight-sleeved jacket belong only to our imagination.

Let us hear the main idea again.

The octopus was hiding in plain sight. Its appearance blended with the rocks. At the aquarium, the children learned about its arms, hearts, and camouflage. Pip discovered that flashing lights are not useful when trying to hide.

Remember our listening question? Why was the octopus difficult to see at first?

Here is the answer. Its appearance blended with the rocks around it. That kind of disguise is called camouflage.
