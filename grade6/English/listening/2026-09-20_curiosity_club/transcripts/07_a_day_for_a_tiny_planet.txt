The Curiosity Club. Episode 7. A Day for a Tiny Planet.

Here is something to listen for. What movement mainly causes day and night on Earth?

Ready? Let us find out.

Leo draws a picture of the Sun going to bed. It wears pajamas and carries a toothbrush.

Is that why it gets dark? Pip asks.

In my picture, yes, says Leo. In real life, probably not.

Ms. Park puts a globe beside a lamp. The lamp represents the Sun. She places a small sticker on the globe for their school.

At first, the sticker faces the lamp. This side is receiving sunlight, Maya says.

Then Ms. Park turns the globe. The sticker moves into the dark side.

Did the lamp turn off? she asks.

No. Our part of the globe turned away, says Leo.

They slowly rotate the globe again. Their school moves back into the light.

Good morning, tiny school, says Pip. I hope your tiny students remembered their homework.

Maya adds another sticker on a distant part of the globe. When our sticker is in the light, that one can be in the dark.

So people do not all eat breakfast at the same moment, Leo says.

Pip is worried. How do I know when to announce lunch?

Use the clock where you are, says Maya.

Leo changes his drawing. Now the Sun stays in place while a little Earth turns beside it. He keeps the pajamas in one corner.

Those are for me, he explains. Learning about night has made me sleepy.

Now, a little more to discover.

Earth rotates. A full turn relative to the Sun takes about twenty-four hours, giving us the familiar day-and-night cycle. The side facing the Sun has daylight; the side facing away has night. Earth's yearly trip around the Sun is a different movement.

Let us hear the main idea again.

A lamp and a globe helped the club model day and night. Their school sticker moved into light and then darkness as the globe turned. The Sun did not need to switch off or go to bed.

Remember our listening question? What movement mainly causes day and night on Earth?

Here is the answer. Earth's rotation causes different parts of its surface to face toward or away from the Sun.
