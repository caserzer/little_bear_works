They visit a botanical garden exhibit about cacao. A photograph shows large pods growing on a tree. Inside the pods are seeds surrounded by pale pulp.

Those seeds are the beginning of chocolate's journey, the guide explains.

Pip looks at the picture. The tree forgot the shiny wrappers.

The guide describes several steps used in making chocolate. Seeds are fermented and dried. They are often roasted, and the inner parts are ground. Different recipes add ingredients such as sugar or milk.

Leo smells a sample of unsweetened cocoa. It smells familiar, but the guide explains that cacao material is naturally bitter. Sweet candy bars get much of their sweetness from added sugar.

So chocolate flavor and sweetness are different things, Maya says.

They learn that cacao has a long history in the Americas, including use in drinks before modern candy bars existed.

Leo expands his drawing into a chain of people and places: growers, processors, makers, transport, and stores.

Pip adds one final box: curious eater.

That is me, says Leo.

Maya smiles. Now your arrow has a story behind it.

Leo agrees, although he still likes the final part of the journey best.

Now, a little more to discover.

Chocolate begins with seeds from cacao pods. Processing develops its familiar flavor, and recipes determine how much sugar, milk, or other ingredients are added. Cacao has a long history in the Americas. A sweet chocolate bar is a finished product with a much longer story behind it.
