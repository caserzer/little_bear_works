The Curiosity Club. Episode 1. The Lunchbox Detective.

Here is something to listen for. What finally helped the children find the owners: the color or the labels?

Ready? Let us find out.

At Maple School, Maya puts a blue lunchbox on the table. Leo puts another blue lunchbox beside it. They look exactly the same.

Is this yours? Maya asks.

I think so, Leo says. He opens it. Inside are rice, carrots, and an egg. That is strange. My dad made me a sandwich.

Then this one is mine, says Maya. But wait. My lunch has noodles today.

Now they have two lunchboxes and no lunch they recognize. Pip, their small classroom robot, rolls over. I have a solution. Eat both lunches.

That is not a solution, says Maya. That is your lunch plan.

Ms. Park asks them to look for clues without eating anything. One box has a tiny star on the bottom. The other has a name under the handle.

This says Sam, Leo reads. So it is his.

A girl named June arrives. The star is mine, she says. My brother drew it.

Maya looks under the table. There is her lunchbox, still inside her backpack. Leo checks his bag, too. His sandwich is there.

We were looking at their lunches, says Maya, but we did not check our own bags.

Pip thinks for a moment. My first clue is that I am still hungry.

You do not eat sandwiches, Leo tells him.

Correct, says Pip. But I can appreciate a good mystery.

Now, a little more to discover.

A clue is something that helps you answer a question. Evidence supports an idea. A blue box is a weak clue when many people have blue boxes. A name label is stronger evidence. Good detectives check their ideas before deciding they are right.

Let us hear the main idea again.

Maya and Leo saw two blue lunchboxes and guessed they were theirs. Labels showed that the boxes belonged to Sam and June. Their own lunches were still in their bags. Looking carefully solved the mystery.
