The Curiosity Club. Episode 4. The Mystery Humming Box.

Here is something to listen for. What happened when Maya gently stopped the rubber band from moving?

Ready? Let us find out.

A strange humming sound comes from the music room. Maya stops outside the door. Is someone singing inside a box?

Leo listens. It sounds like a very small monster with a cold.

They knock and enter. Their music teacher is holding an empty tissue box with rubber bands stretched around it.

Meet my new instrument, she says.

Pip looks into the box. No monster detected.

The teacher gently plucks a rubber band. It moves back and forth so quickly that its middle looks blurry. The box makes a soft musical sound.

That movement is called vibration, she explains. The moving band makes the air around it vibrate, too.

Maya touches the band gently. The movement stops, and the sound stops.

Leo tries a tighter band. It makes a higher sound. He tries again, listening carefully.

Can we play a song? Maya asks.

You can try, says the teacher, but first listen to the notes you have.

Their first song is not beautiful. Pip calls it A Robot Falling Down the Stairs. Their second song is better.

Maya hums along and puts a hand lightly on her throat. She feels a tiny movement.

My voice makes vibrations, too!

Pip records the concert. He saves it under Music, then thinks for a moment and adds a second label: Very Interesting Noise.

Now, a little more to discover.

Sound begins with vibration. Moving objects can make nearby air vibrate, and those changes travel to our ears. Vocal folds in your throat vibrate when you speak. A tighter string usually makes a higher note when its other important features stay the same.

Let us hear the main idea again.

The mystery sound came from a rubber-band instrument. When the band vibrated, the children heard a note. When Maya stopped the movement, the note stopped. They discovered that their own voices involve vibration, too.
