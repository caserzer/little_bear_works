The Curiosity Club. Episode 21. The Puddle's Long Vacation.

Ready? Let us find out.

After rain, a puddle sits beside the playground. Leo draws a chalk line around its edge. Later, the puddle is smaller.

Someone is stealing our puddle, he announces.

Pip begins interviewing the nearby plants. They remain suspiciously quiet.

Maya suggests another explanation. Some water may be soaking into the ground, and some may be evaporating into the air.

Ms. Park helps them think about the water cycle. Liquid water can become water vapor. The vapor is a gas that we cannot see with our eyes.

Then are clouds invisible? Leo asks.

Clouds are visible because they contain tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals, Ms. Park explains. Those are different from invisible water vapor.

They draw a pretend journey for one drop: puddle, air, cloud, rain, stream. Ms. Park reminds them that real water does not always follow that exact route. Some stays underground, in ice, or in other places for a long time.

Pip gives the imaginary drop a suitcase.

Where is it going next? he asks.

We do not know, says Maya. Our drawing shows one possible journey.

By afternoon, the chalk line surrounds mostly dry pavement.

Leo puts up a tiny sign: Puddle away on business.

Maya adds: Water still on Earth. Destination uncertain.

Pip decides that being a drop sounds exciting, but carrying a suitcase while evaporating would be difficult.

Now, a little more to discover.

Water moves among air, land, oceans, rivers, and other stores. Evaporation changes liquid water into vapor. Condensation changes vapor into tiny liquid droplets. Water can return as rain or snow. The water cycle has many routes, rather than one fixed circular trip for every drop.
