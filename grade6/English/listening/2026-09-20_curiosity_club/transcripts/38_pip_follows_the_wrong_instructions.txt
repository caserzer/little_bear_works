The Curiosity Club. Episode 38. Pip Follows the Wrong Instructions.

Here is something to listen for. Why did Pip miss the star on the first attempt?

Ready? Let us find out.

Maya draws a grid on a large sheet of paper. A star marks the destination. Pip will follow a short program to reach it.

Leo writes: Move forward two squares. Turn right. Move forward one square.

Pip follows the instructions and arrives beside a picture of a banana.

That is not the star, Maya says.

Pip checks his record. I followed every instruction.

They examine the starting position. Leo imagined Pip facing the top of the page, but Pip was facing left.

We forgot to specify the starting direction, Maya realizes.

They add it and run the same sequence again. This time, Pip reaches the star.

Next, Leo writes a longer program. One turn is wrong, so Pip ends up at the banana again.

The banana is very popular today, says Pip.

Instead of rewriting everything at random, the children trace each step with a finger. They find the incorrect turn and change it.

That process of finding and fixing problems is called debugging, Ms. Park explains.

Maya asks whether a computer always understands what a person really intends.

It carries out the operations its system is built to perform, says Ms. Park. Clear instructions and checking results still matter.

Pip completes the route at last. He requests one final instruction: Celebrate politely.

Leo writes it more precisely: Ring your bell once.

Pip rings twice.

Everyone looks at him.

A joke, he says. Please do not debug my sense of humor.

Now, a little more to discover.

An algorithm is a sequence of steps for carrying out a task. The starting conditions can affect its result. Debugging means locating and correcting problems in a program. Tracing one step at a time helps connect what the instructions say with what actually happens.

Let us hear the main idea again.

The first route failed because the starting direction was unclear. A later route failed because one turn was wrong. The children checked the steps, found each problem, and corrected it.

Here is the answer. The children had not specified the starting direction, so Pip began facing a different way from the one Leo imagined.
