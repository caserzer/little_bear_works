The Curiosity Club. Episode 15. The Camel's Secret Backpack.

Here is something to listen for. What is stored in a camel's hump?

Ready? Let us find out.

Maya is packing for a day trip. She puts water, lunch, and a hat in her bag. Pip shows her a picture of a camel.

This animal has a built-in backpack, he says.

Leo remembers something he once heard. Does it keep water in its hump?

At the zoo, the educator answers their question. A camel's hump stores fat. That fat is an energy reserve when food is hard to find.

So it is more like stored food energy than a water bottle, Maya says.

Exactly. Camels still need to drink water.

They watch a camel walk slowly across its enclosure. Its broad feet spread its weight over the ground.

Pip examines his narrow wheels. I might need a different design for soft sand.

The educator points out more features that help camels in dry environments. The children learn that several features work together. A single hump does not explain everything.

Leo looks at Maya's backpack. You have water, food, and a hat. Three separate problems, three useful things.

Pip puts a charger, a map, and a photograph of a sandwich in his own travel bag.

Why the photograph? Maya asks.

It weighs less than a sandwich, he says.

That works for you, Leo replies. I would prefer the real thing.

They leave with a better question: What does each feature help an animal do?

Now, a little more to discover.

Camel humps store fat, which can serve as an energy reserve. They are not tanks of liquid drinking water. Camels have several adaptations that help them live in dry places, including ways to conserve water. An adaptation is a feature that helps an organism in its environment.

Let us hear the main idea again.

The children corrected the idea that camel humps are water tanks. A hump stores fat for energy. They also learned to ask how different body features help an animal meet different needs.
