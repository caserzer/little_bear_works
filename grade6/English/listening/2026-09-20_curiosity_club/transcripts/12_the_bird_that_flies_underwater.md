## 完整音频原文

The Curiosity Club. Episode 12. The Bird That Flies Underwater.

Here is something to listen for. Does a penguin stop being a bird because it cannot fly?

Ready? Let us find out.

Leo is making an animal quiz. His first rule says: All birds can fly.

Maya points to a penguin in a nature video. What about that one?

It is swimming, Leo says. Perhaps it is a fish wearing a jacket.

Pip checks his picture library. Jacket detected. Fish status uncertain.

Ms. Park explains that penguins are birds. They have feathers, breathe air, and lay eggs. Their wings are shaped for moving through water instead of flying through air.

The penguin in the video dives, turns, and speeds after a fish. On land, it had looked awkward. Underwater, it looks quick and graceful.

It almost seems to fly down there, Maya says.

That is a useful description, says Ms. Park, as long as we remember it is swimming.

Leo changes his quiz rule. Many birds fly, but some do not.

Then he adds another question. Do all penguins live on Antarctic ice?

Ms. Park shows them penguins on a rocky coast far from Antarctica. Different species live in different places.

Pip tapes paper flippers onto his sides.

Do not try swimming, Maya tells him. You are a classroom robot.

I know, says Pip. These are for my very dry penguin dance.

Leo writes the final quiz question: Which member of our club should definitely stay out of the pool?

Now, a little more to discover.

Penguins are flightless birds with wings adapted for swimming. Their feathers and body features help them live in their environments. Penguin species do not all live on Antarctic ice. Being a bird does not require the ability to fly through the air.

Let us hear the main idea again.

Leo's rule that all birds fly needed a correction. Penguins are birds, but their wings work as flippers underwater. The club learned to check a category using several features instead of one familiar example.

Here are three useful expressions. You can listen, or say them with me.

It can swim, but it cannot fly.
It can swim, but it cannot fly.

This one is different from that one.
This one is different from that one.

Does every bird do that?
Does every bird do that?

Remember our listening question? Does a penguin stop being a bird because it cannot fly?

Here is the answer. No. Penguins are birds even though their wings are adapted for swimming rather than flight in the air.

And here is a question just for your imagination. Can you think of another group whose members do not all do the same thing?

There is more than one possible answer. Keep wondering, and see you next time.
