The Curiosity Club. Episode 2. Pip and the Upside-Down Library.

Here is something to listen for. Why was sorting only by color a problem?

Ready? Let us find out.

The school library needs help. Books are on tables, chairs, and even a windowsill. Pip is excited. I can sort them, he says.

When Maya returns, every shelf looks beautiful. Red books are together. Blue books are together. One green book sits beside a green plant.

Where is the book about sharks? she asks.

What color is it? Pip replies.

I do not remember.

Then we have a small problem.

Leo wants a story by his favorite author. Pip asks whether the cover is dark blue or light blue. Leo does not know that either.

Ms. Park brings three books to the table. One is about whales. One is about the Moon. One is a story about a whale visiting the Moon.

They all have blue covers, Maya says, but their subjects are different.

The librarian explains their system. Stories are in one section. Books that explain real things are in another. Signs and labels help readers find smaller groups.

Let me help you, Leo says to Pip. They check the labels together and move the books. The shark book joins the animal books. The Moon book joins the space books.

Where do I put the plant? Pip asks.

By the window, says the librarian.

Pip adds a little sign: Not a book. Please do not return it through the book slot.

Now, a little more to discover.

Sorting means putting things into groups using a rule. You can sort the same objects in different ways: by size, color, or purpose. A useful system depends on what you need to find. Libraries use organized systems and labels so that other people can follow the rules, too.

Let us hear the main idea again.

Pip sorted books by color. The shelves looked nice, but readers could not easily find a subject or an author. The children followed the library's labels instead. A good sorting rule helps people do a job.

Remember our listening question? Why was sorting only by color a problem?

Here is the answer. Readers often knew a subject or an author, but they did not remember the color of the cover.
