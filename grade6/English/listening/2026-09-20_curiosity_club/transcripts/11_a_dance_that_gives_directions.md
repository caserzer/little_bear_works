The Curiosity Club. Episode 11. A Dance That Gives Directions.

Here is something to listen for. What useful information can a waggle dance share?

Ready? Let us find out.

The club watches a beekeeper's video. A honeybee moves across a crowded surface inside a hive. It walks forward, wiggles, and turns.

That bee is having a party, Leo says.

Perhaps, says Maya, but the others seem to be listening with their feet.

The beekeeper explains that a returning worker can use a waggle dance to share information about food. The movements give other bees clues about direction and distance.

Pip immediately begins a dance. He moves left, turns twice, and rolls backward into a cushion.

What were you saying? asks Maya.

The snack table is near the door.

We need your dance dictionary first, Leo says.

They play a human direction game. One clap means move one step. A raised right hand means turn right. Before starting, everyone agrees on the rules.

Maya follows Leo's signals and reaches a paper flower. Pip follows them and reaches the same flower.

Now we understand each other, Maya says.

The beekeeper reminds them that their invented game is not an exact copy of a bee's dance. It is just a way to think about communication.

Leo invents a new signal: both hands on his stomach.

I know that one, says Maya. You are hungry.

Pip adds it to his dictionary. Some messages, he concludes, are easier than others.

Now, a little more to discover.

Honeybee workers can communicate information about a food source through a waggle dance. Its pattern carries clues about where the food is. Bees also use other signals, including smells. Their dance is an animal communication system, not a human language translated into tiny steps.
